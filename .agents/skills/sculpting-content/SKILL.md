# Sculpting Content

Turn broad intent into publication-ready content by letting the Navigator react to increasingly concrete artifacts while the Driver protects evidence, settled rulings, and editorial coherence.

The method is progressive sculpture, not one-shot drafting. Do not force the Navigator to specify an abstract structure upfront when concrete alternatives or a current full draft would make judgment easier.

## Roles

- The **Navigator** owns purpose, audience judgment, voice, taste, trade-offs, and acceptance. Treat short reactions to concrete material as meaningful rulings.
- The **Driver** acquires evidence, exposes choices, tracks what is settled, produces complete artifacts, checks coherence, and implements accepted content. Do not silently become the owner of product or editorial direction.

## Operating Rules

1. Separate facts, proposals, and settled rulings.
2. Ground public claims in authoritative sources. Never turn aspiration into present-tense capability.
3. Prefer one evolving complete artifact over disconnected fragments once enough shape exists.
4. Resolve consequential editorial questions one at a time when the Navigator benefits from focused judgment.
5. Do not repeatedly reopen settled choices unless new evidence creates a real contradiction.
6. Do not freeze an outline merely because one was proposed. Structure remains provisional until it survives concrete reading.
7. Do not mix copy changes into visual-design comparisons unless copy itself is the axis under review.
8. Keep production untouched until the Navigator accepts the direction, unless the user explicitly asks for direct implementation.
9. Preserve accepted content and the reasoning needed for future work in durable repository artifacts.
10. Keep ceremony proportional: an article may need no browser studies; a public site usually does.

## Core Editorial Workflow

### 1. Orient to intent and evidence

Establish only what is needed to begin:

- intended reader;
- desired effect on that reader;
- relationship to surrounding content or a series;
- voice or style constraints already known;
- authoritative sources and evidence boundaries;
- claims that are proven, qualified, future-facing, or currently unknown.

Read relevant sources before inventing copy. For cross-project subjects, ask source projects for evidence-grounded briefs or inspect their authoritative repositories. The publication repository owns the draft; source repositories own technical truth.

Do not demand answers to every editorial question before producing useful material. Record uncertainty and move to a concrete first surface.

### 2. Establish guardrails

Maintain a concise list of settled constraints such as:

- canonical names and capitalization;
- audience and voice;
- forbidden or disliked constructions;
- tense and honesty rules;
- evidence standards;
- real-link and publication boundaries;
- accepted bold claims that should not be diluted with irrelevant caveats.

Carry these guardrails into later reviews. Do not let reviewers relitigate them as generic preferences.

### 3. Create the first concrete content surface

Depending on how much is known, produce one of:

- a content inventory with competing theses;
- two or three genuinely different narrative dispositions;
- a provisional full outline with representative passages;
- a complete rough draft.

Prefer the smallest artifact that enables the Navigator to react meaningfully. When comparing alternatives, explain the consequential distinction briefly and keep incidental differences out.

For an article series, settle the series thesis and reader journey before deeply outlining every article. Take one article through the complete workflow before over-planning the rest; feed what is learned back into series conventions.

### 4. Sculpt through focused reactions

Use the Navigator’s reactions to reshape the artifact. Translate reactions into explicit working rulings, but do not exaggerate them into permanent universal rules without confirmation.

When a review raises several consequential edits:

1. separate required corrections from optional improvements;
2. present one focused issue with enough surrounding text to judge it;
3. offer concrete replacements when useful;
4. record the ruling;
5. update the evolving whole;
6. continue to the next issue.

Batch mechanical or obviously noncontroversial corrections. Do not make the Navigator approve punctuation one mark at a time.

### 5. Support `snapshot`

When the Navigator says `snapshot`, return only the currently settled content in intended reading order:

- title and headings;
- prose;
- diagrams, examples, commands, quotations, and calls to action that belong in the piece;
- explicit placeholders only where unresolved material must remain visible.

Exclude:

- discussion and rationale;
- rejected alternatives;
- review commentary;
- new unsolicited proposals;
- a changelog of how the content evolved.

A snapshot is the clean acceptance surface, not necessarily the final freeze. Label unresolved areas honestly rather than smoothing over them.

### 6. Run evidence and editorial review

Review the complete current artifact against both editorial quality and source truth.

Check for:

- unsupported or overbroad claims;
- present tense used for work still in development;
- factual statements inconsistent with authoritative repositories;
- jargon before definition;
- internal vocabulary leaking into public prose without need;
- unclear referents or missing transitions;
- repeated slogans, metaphors, conclusions, or examples;
- generic exposition where a concrete example would teach better;
- caveats that obscure the thesis without protecting truth;
- commands or links that do not work in the context claimed;
- missing reader payoff;
- mismatch with settled voice and guardrails.

When using a reviewer or Oracle, provide the complete current artifact, objectives, guardrails, and authoritative evidence locations. Ask for:

- a verdict: ready, ready with small edits, or not ready;
- required edits, preferably current text → replacement;
- optional improvements, clearly separated;
- factual uncertainties requiring verification.

A reviewer advises; the Navigator rules. Do not accept generic reviewer taste over explicit settled direction.

### 7. Canonicalize accepted content

Once the content is sufficiently settled for its next stage, write it to one canonical repository file. State the boundary explicitly: presentation may change hierarchy and rendering, but must not silently rewrite accepted copy.

For articles, use the repository’s canonical content format and location. Preserve draft/publication status honestly. Do not fabricate dates, destinations, images, demonstrations, or links.

## Optional Visual-Presentation Branch

Use this branch only when the task includes visual presentation and the Navigator benefits from choosing by looking rather than describing design abstractly.

### 1. Build disposable complete studies

- Keep studies outside production.
- Use the complete canonical content, not a sample. Reading rhythm cannot be judged from a hero fragment.
- Start with a small number of meaningfully different dispositions, such as editorial, manifesto, or essay.
- Let the Navigator select the reading system before refining details.

Do not invent decorative product evidence. Unqualified diagrams, screenshots, graphs, or demonstrations can falsely imply capability.

### 2. Vary one axis at a time

After a direction is selected, freeze unrelated dimensions and compare one axis per pass, such as:

- hierarchy and reading rhythm;
- typography;
- color palette;
- light/dark treatment;
- density or spacing;
- illustration treatment using real qualified assets.

Do not present repeated three-way choices after the direction is already clear. Refine one accepted direction unless a genuine fork remains.

### 3. Provide a live inspection route

Serve studies through the project’s supported preview mechanism. Give direct links to relevant states where possible. The Driver verifies the artifact before asking the Navigator to inspect it.

For web presentation, scale validation to the project and risk, typically including:

- desktop and mobile widths;
- light and dark modes when supported;
- accessibility checks;
- no unintended overflow;
- structural sanity such as one H1 and unique IDs;
- no console errors;
- complete content present.

### 4. Promote only the accepted direction

After explicit acceptance:

- implement the chosen direction in production;
- remove study-only controls and discarded decoration;
- preserve honest unavailable states;
- update acceptance criteria when the studies changed what “good” means;
- run the repository’s complete relevant validation route;
- retain reviewable evidence when visual or interactive behavior matters.

## Durable Handoff

Make the result survivable without the conversation:

- keep canonical content in its owning repository;
- record durable editorial or design decisions where that repository expects them;
- add scoped agent guidance only for constraints future sessions truly need;
- preserve accepted/rejected conclusions before deleting disposable studies;
- keep source provenance for factual claims;
- ensure ownership links and publication state remain coherent.

Do not preserve every conversational turn. Preserve the settled content, governing constraints, consequential decisions, evidence sources, and unresolved questions.

## Failure Modes to Prevent

- Writing polished copy before understanding the intended reader effect.
- Asking the Navigator to design through abstract adjectives alone.
- Showing partial visual studies that conceal the complete reading rhythm.
- Changing copy and design simultaneously, making feedback ambiguous.
- Treating a reviewer’s generic taste as authority over the Navigator.
- Repeating all context in every response instead of maintaining a clean evolving artifact.
- Weakening a strong truthful thesis with implementation caveats that belong elsewhere.
- Making future capability sound shipped.
- Inventing links, screenshots, commands, or evidence.
- Moving into production before the direction is accepted.
- Leaving the accepted result dependent on one thread’s memory.

## Completion Surface

At a meaningful checkpoint, report:

- what content is now settled;
- what remains deliberately unresolved;
- which claims were verified and against what sources;
- where the canonical artifact lives;
- what changed in presentation, if applicable;
- what validation ran;
- the next focused Navigator judgment, if one is needed.

Keep the report approval-oriented. The artifact itself is the primary review surface.
