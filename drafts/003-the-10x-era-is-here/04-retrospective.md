# Retrospective: Agentic Value Throughput

Completed August 18, 2026 after the final author review.

## Outcome

The complete working draft was strong. I could recognize my thoughts,
experience, criticism, and intended movement from concrete examples to the
larger argument. My review changed substantial prose without changing the
article's center.

The process cost too much attention. The dependency graph helped discover the
argument, but it became an interface I had to operate. Version history was
represented by new files instead of Git diffs, so reviewing a change often
required reading another complete document.

## What worked

1. Free voice notes produced the real material and preserved first-person
   authority.
2. The graph exposed shared premises and helped relocate software economics to
   the center.
3. Writing complete proposals made the argument judgeable. The strongest
   decisions came from reading prose, not inspecting planning artifacts.
4. Concrete-to-abstract editing improved the opening, historical bridge,
   Toyota turn, and personal example.
5. Portal review made comments local and visual. Exact change reports prevented
   another complete reread.

## What cost too much

1. The canonical graph reached 240 claims. Generated layers, an SVG, trees,
   workbenches, and reading-order branches exposed the planning model instead of
   compressing it for my judgment.
2. Questions referred to IDs such as `T06` without restating their assertions.
   I had to reopen large documents to understand the decision.
3. Two planning files were presented without a clear review owner. I had to ask
   whether to read the tree or the workbench.
4. The process created 81 draft files, including 24 author-review records and
   many complete prose snapshots. Changes across different paths could not be
   reviewed as normal Git diffs.
5. The Monday-night review degraded into one-word merge and option decisions.
   That was evidence that the agent had transferred synthesis back to me.

## Changes for the next article

1. Keep one active file per stage. Cumulative knowledge does not require
   cumulative files.
2. Use the graph privately and optionally. Compress it into no more than seven
   contextual argumentative moves before asking for my judgment.
3. Draft the complete article as soon as its center and reader movement are
   clear. Use prose to correct the plan.
4. Establish a Git baseline before author review. Edit the same file, show the
   exact diff, and commit accepted work at section, pass, or review-session
   boundaries.
5. Create parallel draft files only for alternatives I explicitly want to
   compare side by side.
6. Ask direct, self-contained questions. Never require me to recover an ID from
   another document.
7. Keep Portal review. It was the clearest and fastest interface for me in the
   process.

## Cleanup

The retrospective reduces this draft directory from 81 files to nine durable
artifacts. Raw independent transcripts, the consolidated source notes, brief,
canonical graph, selected reading order, pre-review draft, and this
retrospective remain. Generated graph views, superseded planning projections,
pass reports, review records, and prose snapshots are removed.
