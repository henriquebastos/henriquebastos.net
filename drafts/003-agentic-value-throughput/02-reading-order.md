# Reading order: Economics first

Status: Selected route after Henrique compared the complete economics-first and
Toyota-first proposals. This route produced the pre-review draft in
`03-draft.md`.

## Reader movement

### 1. The multiplier question receives an economic answer

Open with "ten times more what?" Software construction and maintenance are
investment. Code is inventory. The economic leverage begins when one running
system creates value across users and time. Agents compress the investment
cycle, but increasing the volume of the cheaper input does not establish more
value.

The reader receives the article's center immediately: the opportunity is not
raw code speed but using compressed time to reach and strengthen running value.

### 2. The old pipeline becomes sympathetic context

Compress the inherited system into one movement. Slow human implementation and
the risk of building the wrong software made tickets, branches, pull requests,
CI, and review coherent. Branches isolated work by creating alternate
timelines, which later had to be reconciled. Faster construction does not erase
integration or entropy.

### 3. The economic mistake becomes organizational pressure

Companies have fragmented the path from engineering investment to experienced
value. Code, tickets, features, and roadmap progress remain the visible local
outputs, so leadership asks agents to multiply them. Developers absorb the
review, coordination, and accountability burden.

Typecraft's account gives that structural failure a human body. The anger lands
on leadership competence: explaining the proxy does not absolve leaders from
seeing and redesigning the system.

### 4. Toyota changes the scaling relationship

Give the watched loom and Sakichi Toyoda's redesign their full turn. Continuous
watching couples machine capacity to human attention. Detection, stopping, and
signaling move people toward judgment, repair, and production-system
improvement. Safe parallelism becomes a result of redesign rather than a target
imposed on workers.

### 5. Henrique's experience produces the positive abstraction

Enter through the project that required a strategy new to the company. Six
disposable approaches exposed the solution space. The project took roughly two
months instead of an estimated six. The important result was not raw speed:
wide exploration produced the understanding required for deeper architecture,
operations, platform integration, instrumentation, and scaling.

Expand from the example. One narrow value intention can contain parallel agent
research, measurement, experiments, and implementation while human attention
remains focused on design and judgment. AI capacity funds better solution
selection and higher quality instead of more unrelated output.

Aaron Francis becomes a supporting resonance after Henrique's experience, not
the basis of the model.

### 6. Depth reaches running value

Carry the same narrow intention through integration, controlled exposure,
observation, and repair. A merged feature has not created value merely by
joining the repository. Instrumentation and production evidence belong to the
first real version. The running result gives later parallel work something to
strengthen.

Use only enough testing and tooling detail to make the quality commitment
concrete. Do not turn the movement into a catalog.

### 7. Leadership moves up to unlock quality and the future

Close with the missing higher-level responsibility. Tickets may be too low to
contain the loop, while abstract business goals remain too high to bound it.
Shared executable collaboration, continuous evidence, and production-reactive
agents are brief contours of a system still being invented.

The final emphasis belongs to leadership. Moving above code output and agent
utilization is what can unlock both higher quality now and the future
production system later.

## Turn and reversal

**Turn:** Toyota changes the problem from increasing supervised output to
redesigning where human judgment enters.

**Reversal:** The opening asks what agents can multiply. The close returns the
multiplier to leadership: AI capacity becomes leverage only when leaders design
the complete value-producing system instead of demanding more inventory.
